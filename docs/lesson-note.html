Cell Structure and Organisation

Understanding the basic unit of life and the functions of its components.

Learning objectives

  • Define a cell and explain why it is the basic unit of life.
  • Identify major parts of plant and animal cells.
  • Describe the function of each cell structure.
  • Compare plant and animal cells accurately.

Introduction

All living organisms are made up of cells. Some organisms consist of only one cell, while complex organisms contain trillions of specialised cells working together. The cell is therefore described as the basic structural and functional unit of life.

Major cell structures

Cell membrane

A thin boundary that controls what enters and leaves the cell.

Cytoplasm

A jelly-like material where many chemical reactions take place.

Nucleus

The control centre containing genetic information.

Mitochondrion

The site of aerobic respiration and energy release.

Chloroplast

Contains chlorophyll for photosynthesis in plant cells.

Vacuole

Stores cell sap and supports the shape of a plant cell.

Plant and animal cells

Both plant and animal cells have a cell membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus, and mitochondria. Plant cells additionally have a cellulose cell wall, chloroplasts, and a large permanent vacuole. These features support the plant and allow it to produce food through photosynthesis.

Class activity

  1. Draw and label one plant cell and one animal cell.
  2. State three similarities and three differences between them.
  3. Explain what could happen if the cell membrane stopped working correctly.

Revision questions

  1. What is the basic unit of life?
  2. Which cell structure controls cell activities?
  3. Name two structures found in plant cells but not animal cells.
  4. State the function of the mitochondrion.
